*From the archive. In August 2023 I was in conversation with Indy Johar, co-founder of Dark Matter Labs, for the first of four seminars in the Planetary Civics Seminar Series, a partnership between RMIT and Dark Matter Labs. Indy hadn't read the questions in advance, so what follows is an unscripted hour. The full recording is above.*

Indy trained as an architect and found that form follows money rather than function. The example that stayed with me was the street tree. To a council it is an insurance cost and a maintenance cost, while its cooling, its help with flooding and its benefits to health go unpriced. So, in Indy's words, the built environment is "structured and coded by our accounting logics", and changing places means changing what gets counted.

From there the conversation moved to the courage to re-examine ourselves, to planetary entanglement ("there is nothing local about me"), to machines as part of that entanglement rather than outside it, and to the limits of democracy narrowed to the vote. Late in the hour Indy turned to a freedom to care: "not a freedom to escape, but a freedom to relate".
