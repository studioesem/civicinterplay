*A position paper by Dr Sarah Barns, January 2025, written as part of a Vice-Chancellor's Senior Research Fellowship at RMIT University's Centre for Urban Research. With thanks to Stephen Johnston and Professor Iain Buchan, Civic Health Innovation Labs, University of Liverpool.*

**[Download the paper (PDF, 14 pages)](/towards-civic-ai-barns-2025.pdf)**

## Key points

- **Open data has not closed the loop.** Governments made public data freely available to spur innovation, but its beneficiaries have been obligation-free, and public agencies have become dependent on the platforms built on it.
- **The surveillance critique misses something.** Concern for personal privacy says little about the value lost to society when public data, and publicly funded research, flow into private learning systems.
- **Institutions need to align their information ecosystems with their mission.** For public and mission-led organisations, data governance is now an institutional priority: whose learning systems are their data training?
- **Data only becomes valuable inside a learning system.** Civil society needs learning systems of its own to evidence what its work contributes to public outcomes. Civic AI builds on the citizen science movement to do this.
- **Liverpool shows one way.** The University of Liverpool's Civic Health Innovation Labs and the Liverpool City Region Civic Data Cooperative treat citizens as stewards of their health data, through evidence pipelines, custodians and temporary permissions.
- **Civic AI activation zones.** The paper proposes extending the innovation precinct model to test civic learning systems in place, building on Barcelona's New Data Deal and the DECODE project, and scaling through "mirrored sandboxes".
- **AI does not have to be centralised.** Most health outcomes are shaped by where and how people live. When AI is locally owned and democratically governed, it can be optimised for what communities actually want.
