<Callout>

**Australia is building the data centres and renting the mind.** The difference is about to matter.

</Callout>

Listen to the announcements and Australia is having a sovereignty moment. Sovereign compute rising at data centre campuses, a National AI Plan in the language of national capability, government Expectations that invoke data sovereignty and the national interest. The word is everywhere. It is worth slowing down on, because it is quietly doing two jobs at once.

Two things travel under the name sovereign AI. One is the capacity to build the thing yourself: the models, the compute you control, the researchers, and at the far end the chips. The other is the capacity to host someone else's system on your own soil, under your own data-residency law. The first is sovereignty over the mind. The second is sovereignty over the building the mind runs in. Australia has gone almost entirely for the second, and taken to calling it the first.

To make that visible, we map it: every data centre, mine, refinery, and piece of energy and water infrastructure behind AI in Australia. Who owns it, who approved it, and what it draws to keep running, alongside the public debate forming around each site.

[Open the live map](/sovereignties), or read [how the project works](https://studio-esem.notion.site/AI-Sovereignties-project-explainer-38d18df0b77581ab8465dcb65a6628ad).

This is a draft, and a beginning. The question it keeps asking is the one rarely asked at the point of adoption: not whether to use these systems, but what sovereignty their adoption quietly relocates, and to whom.
