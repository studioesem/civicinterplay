<Callout>

Civic Interplay explores acts of civic care, relationality and custodianship in an era of agentic AI and AI sovereignty.

</Callout>

<Callout>

It is a think and do space. It is *all* work in progress.

</Callout>

<Callout>

If times have become strange - *agentic, worrisome* - the nature of enquiry need not be *without play*.

</Callout>

<Callout>

This is not a space where outcomes are pre-determined. Where theory informs action. What is known is no longer, necessarily, the ground on which we act.

</Callout>

<Callout>

**The 'Macro Age' We're Living Through**

We are living through the [Sixth Mass Extinction](https://www.worldwildlife.org/resources/explainers/what-is-the-sixth-mass-extinction-and-what-can-we-do-about-it/), the rise of [AI sovereignty](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2024/11/what-is-sovereign-ai-and-why-is-the-concept-so-appealing-and-fraught/), and a series of [catastrophic climate tipping points](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2025/oct/13/coral-reefs-ice-sheets-amazon-rainforest-tipping-point-global-heating-scientists-report).

</Callout>

<Callout>

**The speech-acts: what have they become?**

Units of attention to be hard-earned, quantified outputs to be reported, inputs to a hyperscaler's model?

</Callout>

<Callout>

*All Media is Training Data*, as Holly Herndon and Mat Dryhurst would say.

</Callout>

<Callout>

The city is an LLM.

</Callout>

<Callout>

**Global Governance?**

Multi-lateral institutions have given way to a planetary AI now governing the logics of information, computation, and increasingly, co-intelligence.

</Callout>

<Callout>

If this is a macro-age, what does it feel like to be a citizen? Does citizenship still matter?

</Callout>

<Callout>

**The macro age we find ourselves in is creating, before our eyes, a different kind of history.**

It's not the kind of history we were taught before. We can no longer say: *And then they lived comfortably ever after.*

We are not so sure of that any more.

</Callout>

<Callout>

**But we can say:** *We are the interface for a new politics of becoming.*

By 'interface', we mean something specific: we are the living, sensing, semi-autonomous connection between the world as it has been and the world as it might become.

</Callout>

<Callout>

Through our daily choices, we are actively training the planetary intelligences that shape material futures.

</Callout>

<Callout>

Which platforms to use, which data to share, which automated systems to trust or resist, what terms and conditions we say yes to, what model we sign our allegiance to.

</Callout>

<Callout>

This is the *role playing* asked of us during these emergent years of the agentic age.

</Callout>

<Callout>

This is the micro politics of *civic interplay.*

</Callout>

<Callout>

Examining the civic interface we are becoming, and what forms of civic action and intention have a role to play in the story of global AI governance.

</Callout>

</CardScroller>

## Beyond legacy models

How to think about AI governance from the ground up.

<CardScroller label="Beyond legacy models">

<Callout>

**The language of digital computing is suffused with legacy models:**  
  
**'Cloud computing'**, as though our data had no weight, no carbon cost, no material infrastructure spanning the earth  
**'Human-centred design'**, as though humans are the only thing that matters in a more-than-human world facing ecological collapse  
**'Artificial intelligence'**, as though it is somehow not real, or not borne of the collective wealth of knowledge  
**'Information design'**, as though designing how information flows were not also political acts

</Callout>

<Callout>

_**What about instead:**_  
Our interconnected lives operate as training grounds for a new kind of planetary intelligence. The question is not whether we'll have planetary-scale AI systems. We already do. The question is what kind of citizens we'll be in relation to them.

</Callout>

<Callout>

_**What about instead:**_  
**The institutions to which we owe our allegiance and identities as citizens are becoming strange.**  
  
It is not only 'governments' as we have known them, but 'governing bodies', extending beyond these formal states, upon which we have become interdependent, in both cognitive and infrastructural ways.

</Callout>
