Civic Interplay / Steward onboarding / Copenhagen

A Steward's First Walk

Attention before the context window. Forty-five minutes, one place, four registers of being, six guides holding six keys.

Draft. Sarah's words go over the top of mine.

The spine

Eight movements, about 45 minutes

One rule holds the whole thing together: no screens until movement six.
002 min

The threshold

Phones into the bag. Watch or paper timer only. Name out loud what each of you is carrying into this, including the thing you would rather not be carrying.

We are going to pay attention to this place before we ask a machine anything about it.

016 min

Listen to now

Standing or sitting, not walking. No talking, no notes. Count sounds on your fingers if you need somewhere to put the attention. Copenhagen offers water, bells, bikes, gulls, wind, ventilation. Hold until the timer, past the point where it gets uncomfortable.

Listen to now. Six minutes. Nothing to produce at the end of it.

026 min

How do we feel this place

Now speak. Each of you names the sense that dominated, the sense that was missing or suppressed, and one thing your body did without being asked. Write only after saying it.

What senses stand out? And which one did this place shut down?

032 min

The fork

Lay the four register cards down. This is the only moment of the walk that is a choice rather than an instruction. Each of you takes one, and says why, in one sentence.

We want to map a complicated terrain with our hearts, our bodies, our minds and our agents. What do you want to speak from first?

0410 min

Walk the register

Follow the three beats on the card you took, out loud, while walking. When you finish your own, pick up the register the other person did not choose and walk that one too. The trap line on each card is the thing to watch for in yourself, not in each other.

058 min

Conjure a guide

Six guides, face down. Draw one, or choose one if the terrain has already told you who it wants. Take the key and perform the act on the card, here, before any of it becomes language for a machine.

Each guide holds a key. The key does not explain the place. It opens a part of it you were not looking at.

068 min

The context window

Phones out. The guide comes into the window with you, and the key is the constraint: the guide asks, it does not answer. Give the agent what you noticed first, then the key, then ask it what you missed. Notice the moment your attention leaves the place and goes into the screen. That moment is the subject.

You are not the guide. You hold the guide's key. Here is what we noticed. Ask us what we did not.

073 min

The return

Screens away again. Two questions, then the capture sheet: what did the agent reach that you could not, and what did you hold that it could not get near. The sheet becomes the first field note.

The four registers

Movement 03, then 04

Register

Heart

What here would you defend, and from what?

  1. The attachment.
  2. The loss you can already name.
  3. Who else holds it.

Trap: feeling standing in for a claim.

Register

Body

What is your body registering that has not yet become words?

  1. The sense that dominated.
  2. The sense that was shut down.
  3. What you moved away from.

Trap: describing the view instead of the sensation.

Register

Mind

What structure do you think is operating here?

  1. The pattern.
  2. The counter-example.
  3. Who the pattern pays.

Trap: arriving with the answer already loaded.

Register

Agent

What would you set a machine looking for, and what will it miss?

  1. The question worth delegating.
  2. The context it cannot have.
  3. What you will check by hand afterwards.

Trap: opening the window before the other three have spoken.

Six guides, six keys

Movement 05, carried into 06

Guide

Ursula Le Guin

Key: the bag

Terrain
What this place holds, rather than what it does.
The act
Name five things this place carries. Then one it refuses to carry.
Bring back
A list, not a story.
In the window
Ask for the container, not the conflict.

Guide

Carlo Rovelli

Key: the interval

Terrain
The durations layered in one site. Tidal, seasonal, budgetary, electoral, a training run.
The act
Name three clocks running here at different speeds. Say which one the place is governed by.
Bring back
Three speeds and their units.
In the window
Ask what changes if the question is asked in a different clock.

Guide

Walter Benjamin

Key: the fragment

Terrain
The outmoded and the discarded. The past lodged inside the present.
The act
Pick up one thrown-away thing. Read it as a document.
Bring back
The object itself, in your pocket.
In the window
Give the agent the fragment before you give it the frame.

Guide

David Graeber

Key: the IOU

Terrain
Obligation. The arbitrary wearing the clothes of the necessary.
The act
Name one rule everybody here obeys that somebody made up.
Bring back
The rule, and who it pays.
In the window
Ask how else it could have been made, and who would have had to agree.

Guide

Elinor Ostrom

Key: the boundary

Terrain
Rules in use, as distinct from rules on paper.
The act
Draw the edge of one shared thing here. Then find out who watches it.
Bring back
A boundary and a monitor.
In the window
Hold what you drew against her eight design principles and see which are missing.
The name
She and Vincent Ostrom called their Bloomington centre a Workshop, not an institute: artisans, tools, apprentices, things made and repaired in company. It is the closest existing description of what we are doing.

Guide

James Lovelock

Key: the instrument

Terrain
Metabolism. Heat, water, air, load. The heat you are standing in has a source, and in Denmark some of it comes back as district heating.
The act
If you could read only this place's breath, how would you know it is alive?
Bring back
One flow you could actually measure.
In the window
Ask what a probe passing overhead would read in the atmosphere here.

A pairing, if the terrain turns to governance. Draw Graeber and Ostrom together. One key opens what is arbitrary and could be otherwise, the other opens what has endured because somebody maintains it. The argument between them is the useful part.
